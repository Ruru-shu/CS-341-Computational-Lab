Explanation:
1. Standard polynomial monomials {1, x, x^2, x^3} lead to a Hankel normal matrix
   where elements grow rapidly (sum x^(m+k)), making rows nearly linearly dependent.
   This causes severe ill-conditioning and makes matrix inversion susceptible to floating-point roundoff.
2. In contrast, modified Chebyshev polynomials {phi_0, phi_1, phi_2, phi_3} form an
   orthogonal basis on [0, 1]. The inner products sum phi_m(x_i)*phi_k(x_i) approx delta_mk,
   yielding a well-conditioned, diagonally dominant matrix.
3. Consequently, the condition number for Chebyshev basis is orders of magnitude smaller,
   ensuring robust, numerically stable matrix inversion via Cholesky decomposition.
